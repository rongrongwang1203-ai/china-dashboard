Reading the evidence

  • No depreciation pressure in the data. The RMB has appreciated about 9% against the dollar since January 2025 (monthly average 7.30 → 6.68 CNY/USD), and the real effective rate has recovered from 86.2 (2025-06) to 92.9, roughly its level before early 2025 (92.4).
  • Relative PPP points to further RMB strength. With inflation of 0.9% in China and 3.7% in the US, relative PPP implies the RMB should appreciate by about 2.8% a year over the medium run (class: St+1/St = (1+π*)/(1+π)); it is a medium-run guide, not a one-year forecast.
  • External balance is supportive. The current account surplus was 3.7% of GDP in 2025 (IMF projects 4.0% for 2026) and reserves are up 6.7% since December 2024.
  • Offsetting factors: the 1-year LPR (3.0%) is 0.88 pp below the US policy rate, so RMB is the cheaper funding currency, and IMF growth projections slow from 5.0% (2025) to 4.5% (2026) and 4.0% (2027).
  • For a US firm: by covered interest parity (R − R* ≈ 0.88%) the RMB trades at a forward premium of roughly 0.9%. Under a random-walk forecast, selling RMB forward on receivables earns that premium and buying RMB forward for payables costs it. Hedge on exposure, not on this view: the random walk beats most exchange-rate forecasts.
